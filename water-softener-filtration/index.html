Facts first

Start with what the utility already tells you

Seacoast Utility Authority publishes an annual water quality report. The 2025 report describes a treatment train of nanofiltration and low-pressure reverse osmosis at the Hood Road Membrane Water Plant, chloramine at 3.7 ppm on average and a sodium reading of 35.5 ppm, with the maximum at 160 ppm. The report lists no hardness figure.

That means we cannot tell you your hardness from the report. The honest approach is to test your own tap or ask the utility. Treatment sized to a number is better than treatment sized to a guess. NSF/ANSI 44 is the standard for softeners, while NSF/ANSI 53 and 58 are the standards for filters and reverse osmosis, so look for the number that matches your goal.

Symptoms

Symptoms and what they might mean

  • White crust on faucets and glasswareMineral deposits. A test shows if hardness is the cause.
  • Chlorine or chloramine tasteA carbon filter can reduce taste. It does not soften water.
  • Brown or orange stainsIron or old galvanized pipe. A test or inspection tells which.
  • Slow flow from fixturesScale or sediment clogging aerators.
  • Dry skin after showeringMany factors, including soap and water temperature. Test before buying equipment.

Testing

How to test your tap

Home test strips give a rough hardness reading. A lab test gives accurate numbers for hardness, iron, pH and more. Ask the utility whether it has hardness data for your service area.

We can discuss the results with you and suggest whether treatment is worthwhile. In some cases the best advice is to do nothing and to clean aerators now and then.

Local note

Softeners and the sewer

Salt-based softeners discharge brine when they regenerate. Some communities restrict that discharge, and sodium in the wastewater matters to some utilities. Ask your utility whether any rules apply to you.

The utility's report lists a sodium level of 35.5 ppm for a 2023 sample, which it attributes to salt water intrusion. That is the water in the pipes, and it is a separate subject from brine from a softener.

Myths

Common myths about water treatment

A device that makes water taste better does not necessarily soften it. A device that removes chlorine does not necessarily remove minerals. A salt-free conditioner changes how scale forms but does not reduce hardness numbers on a test.

Ask what a product is certified to do and for which contaminant. A clear answer is a good sign, and a vague one is a reason to keep shopping.
