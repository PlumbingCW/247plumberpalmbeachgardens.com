Scope

What counts as an installation

Installation includes sinks, faucets, toilets, showers and tubs, dishwashers, washing machines, ice makers, outdoor spigots and new shutoff valves. It also includes the hidden work of rough-in plumbing before walls close during a remodel.

Each fixture has supply, drain and vent requirements. A fixture that looks simple may need a new vent or a wider drain, so we check what is behind the wall before ordering. Appliance manuals set the guidelines for supply and drain connections, and the Florida Building Code, Plumbing governs fixtures and venting.

Checklist

What we confirm before installing

  • Supply and shutoffA working shutoff valve makes later service easier.
  • Drain size and slopeA fixture needs the correct drain size and fall to drain properly.
  • VentingA vent lets air in so traps do not siphon.
  • Water pressureToo high a pressure strains new fixtures.
  • Local codeThe Florida Building Code, Plumbing, 8th Edition (2023) governs the work.

Permits

Remodels and permits

A remodel that moves walls or adds a bathroom will usually involve a permit. Permit rules vary, so check with the Palm Beach Gardens Building Division at 561-799-4210, which accepts permits through an online system.

We coordinate rough-in timing with your contractor so inspectors see open walls when they should. If we do the plumbing, we describe what each inspection looks at.

In Palm Beach Gardens

Installing for Florida homes

Slab homes put drains in concrete, so moving a fixture can mean cutting the floor. We plan fixture locations around existing drains where possible. In two-story homes, venting and water hammer are additional concerns.

Outdoor spigots and irrigation tie-ins need backflow protection. We install the correct device where required and explain why it is there.

Hookups

Appliance hookups we handle

Dishwashers need a hot supply, a drain with a high loop and power. Washing machines need two supply valves and a standpipe with an air gap. Ice makers need a small supply line with a shutoff. Each has quirks, such as the correct drain height, which we confirm before the appliance goes in.

Gas dryers and ranges need a flexible connector, a shutoff and a leak check. If the appliance is new, ask the seller about connector kits so you do not make a second trip.

Preparation

Getting ready for the visit

Clear the space around the sink, closet or laundry area. Tell us about any past leaks or slow drains in that spot. Have the fixture or appliance on site, with its manual, so we can check the rough-in dimensions before we start.

Take a photo of the area under the sink or behind the washer before the visit. It helps us bring the right fittings and avoids a second trip for a missing part.
