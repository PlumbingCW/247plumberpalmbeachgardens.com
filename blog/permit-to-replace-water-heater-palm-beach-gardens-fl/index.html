Is a Permit Needed to Replace a Water Heater in Palm Beach Gardens, FL

Permit rules vary, so the safest step is to ask the Palm Beach Gardens Building Division at 561-799-4210. The city's own page describes how permits are submitted but does not state a specific rule for water heaters.

This guide explains what that means and how to avoid delays.

What we can and cannot say

We are not the permit authority. The building division decides what applies to your address, and its answer can depend on the type of heater, the fuel and whether venting or wiring changes.

What we can do is describe the work clearly so that when you call, the division can answer a specific question. The Florida Building Code is a statewide standard enforced by local building departments, which is why the city division is the right place to ask.

What to have ready when you call

  • Your addressThe division looks up the property.
  • Current heater typeGas, electric, hybrid or tankless.
  • Planned heater typeSame or different from the old one.
  • LocationGarage, closet or attic.
  • Venting or wiring changesMention them if the plan changes.
Local note

The division's contact details

The Building Division is at City Hall, 10500 N Military Trail, with phone 561-799-4210 and email webpermits@pbgfl.gov. Its page describes an online permit system.

If you live in North Palm Beach, the village hall is at 501 US Highway 1 and the number is 561-841-3380. See our North Palm Beach page for more.

Our North Palm Beach page lists the village office to call if you live there.

Source type: City of Palm Beach Gardens Building Division page and Village of North Palm Beach.

Where the city page leaves off

The City's Building Division page lists a phone number, an email address, an online permit system and office hours of 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., Monday to Friday. It lists inspection scheduling from 8 a.m. to 3 p.m.

It does not say whether a like-for-like water heater swap requires a permit. Instead of guessing, call or email the division.

Why it matters

Permit and inspection steps protect you if a heater is installed wrongly. A gas heater with poor venting, for example, can put carbon monoxide in the home. An inspection is a second set of eyes.

It can also matter when you sell. Buyers and insurers sometimes ask about permits for major equipment.

What we do on a replacement

We size the heater, set it on a pan, install a relief valve with a safe discharge, connect gas or power, vent as required and test. See water heater replacement and water heater installation for the full steps.

If the city requires a permit, we tell you before work starts so the timing is clear.

Emergency replacements

When a tank has failed at night, you may not be able to wait for office hours. Call the 24/7 line and we will explain what is possible. The building division answers during business hours, so ask about permits the next day or let us handle the question.

Documents that make a permit smoother

Where a permit applies, the division usually wants basic facts about the job. Have the address, the owner name, the contractor license details, the heater make and model and a short description ready. A clear description speeds a review and avoids a call back.

If a permit is issued, keep the permit card visible until final inspection and keep a copy of the final sign-off with your home records. Insurers and buyers sometimes ask for it later.

Red flags in a replacement quote

  • A contractor who says permits are never neededRules vary, so a blanket claim should prompt a question.
  • No mention of the relief valve dischargeA safe discharge line is a basic safety item.
  • No pan or drain in a closet installA leak will go straight into the floor.
  • Vague venting plans for gas unitsPoor venting is a carbon monoxide risk.
  • Unwillingness to put the scope in writingClear paperwork protects both sides.

What happens at an inspection

Where a permit applies, an inspector typically looks at the installation itself. That includes the relief valve and its discharge pipe and the expansion tank where required. It also covers the venting and gas connection on gas units, the electrical connection on electric units and the support and location of the heater.

Being home or having someone available helps. If an item is wrong, the inspector lists what needs correction, and the contractor fixes it before the final sign-off.

Selling or insuring a home with a replaced heater

Buyers and insurers sometimes ask when major equipment was replaced and whether permits were closed. Keeping the paperwork in one folder answers that fast. A closed permit shows the work was reviewed.

If you bought a home and are unsure whether a past replacement was permitted, ask the Building Division what records exist for the address.
