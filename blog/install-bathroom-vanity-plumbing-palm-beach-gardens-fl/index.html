How to Install Bathroom Vanity Plumbing in Palm Beach Gardens, FL

Installing a vanity looks simple until the old drain does not line up with the new sink. Planning the drain height and trap first prevents most surprises.

This guide covers the plumbing connections and where a plumber helps.

Measure the plumbing first

Before buying a vanity, measure the drain stub height and the supply stub spacing in the wall. The drain should line up with the vanity's open back. Drawers can block a trap, so a vanity made for plumbing in the middle may not fit.

Standard drains are an inch and a quarter or an inch and a half. The rough-in height matters more than the brand. The IPC calls for a minimum 1-1/4 inch trap on a bathroom lavatory, which matches the usual drain size under a vanity.

Step by step

Shut off water

Close the shutoff valves under the old sink.

Remove the old sink

Disconnect supplies and trap and lift the vanity.

Set the new vanity

Level it and attach it to the wall studs.

Install the faucet and drain

Mount the faucet and pop-up drain on the sink before dropping it in.

Connect supplies and trap

Use braided supply lines and a P-trap, tightening by hand and a quarter turn.

Test

Run water, fill the sink and check for drips.

Common mistakes

  • Reusing old supply linesRubber or braided lines wear out. Use new ones.
  • OvertighteningPlastic nuts crack when overtightened.
  • Wrong trap alignmentA crooked trap leaks or gurgles.
  • No shutoff valveAdd one so future service is easy.
  • Ignoring the ventA missing vent causes gurgling.
Local note

Permit and code context

The Florida Building Code, Plumbing, 8th Edition (2023) is the current plumbing code. Replacing a vanity in the same location often falls under simple maintenance, but moving drains or adding fixtures can involve permits.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Palm Beach Gardens Building Division at 561-799-4210 if your project changes the layout.

If your project is in North Palm Beach, the village building department handles permits.

Faucet choices

A faucet that needs three holes needs a sink with three holes. A single-hole faucet fits many sinks. A wall-mounted faucet needs rough-in plumbing inside the wall, which is a bigger project.

If you are replacing the faucet, see faucet repair for fixes and replacements.

Water quality and fixtures

White crust on faucets points to mineral deposits. The local utility report lists no hardness figure, so test your tap before assuming. Cleaning the aerator every few months keeps flow strong either way.

When to call for help

If the drain does not line up, if you find corrosion at the shutoff valves or if the wall behind the vanity is damp, call a plumber. Hidden damage is better found now than later. Start with leak detection if you suspect a slow leak.

Matching a vanity to your wall plumbing

Rough-in plumbing is set before the walls are closed, and the vanity has to fit around it. Measure the drain height from the floor, the distance from the side wall and the spacing between the hot and cold stubs. Take the numbers to the store and compare them with the vanity's back panel layout.

A vanity with a full back and no cutout cannot sit flush over a drain stub. You can cut openings in the back panel with a jigsaw, but measure twice and make sure the cut will not weaken the cabinet.

Tools and parts list

  • Adjustable wrenchesTwo, so you can hold one fitting while turning another.
  • Plumber's tapeUsed on threaded metal fittings.
  • Braided supply linesNew lines for the faucet.
  • P-trap kitIncludes the arm, the bend and slip joints.
  • Silicone sealantFor the backsplash and wall edge.

Why shutoff valves are worth the extra step

A vanity swap is the easiest time to replace tired shutoff valves. If the old ones are stuck, leaking or corroded, they will fail at the worst time. New quarter-turn valves let you stop the water at the sink in a second.

Closing a valve that has not moved in years can crack it, so turn it gently, and be ready to shut the main valve if it starts to weep.

Checking your work

After the install, fill the sink halfway, release it and watch the trap and the drain connections. Run the faucet at full flow for a minute and wipe every joint with a dry paper towel. Check again an hour later and the next morning.

A slow weep at a slip joint usually means the washer is cocked or the nut needs another quarter turn. If a leak persists, take the trap apart, reseat the washers and try again.

Space and height planning

Check that the faucet handle clears the mirror and the wall, and that the sink bowl is not too close to a door swing. Standard vanity heights range from thirty to thirty-six inches. A taller vanity changes where the drain and trap sit relative to the rough-in.

Dry-fit the vanity before attaching it. Mark stud locations, set the cabinet in place and check that the drain stub lines up with the trap you plan to use.
