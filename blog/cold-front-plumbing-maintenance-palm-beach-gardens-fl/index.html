Winter and Cold Front Plumbing Maintenance in Palm Beach Gardens, FL

Hard freezes are rare in Palm Beach Gardens, but cold fronts do occasionally bring lows into the 30s and 40s. Exposed pipes, hose bibs and outdoor equipment are the weak spots.

This guide is short because the fix is mostly small, cheap steps done before the cold arrives.

How cold gets here

South Florida winters are mild, and most winters never reach a hard freeze. But news and weather service forecasts sometimes show lows in the 30s and 40s for a night or two. Pipes outside the building envelope can be at risk when that happens.

The risk is highest for water that sits still in an exposed pipe or fixture overnight. According to National Weather Service freeze guidance, outdoor pipes and irrigation are among the first things to protect.

Local note

What the forecast says versus what you see

Local news and National Weather Service reports occasionally show lows in the 30s and 40s for South Florida during strong cold fronts. Those reports are about forecasts for the region and not guarantees for your street.

Palm Beach Gardens also averages about 66 inches of rain on about 113 days a year, so winter damp is as likely as winter cold. Watch your forecast and act the day before a cold night.

Waterfront homes in North Palm Beach face wind as well as cold, so cover outdoor fittings there too.

Source type: National Weather Service and local news reports and city climate summary.

Before the cold night

  • Cover hose bibsUse an insulated hose bib cover and disconnect hoses.
  • Protect exposed pipesWrap pipes on outer walls, garages and near vents with pipe insulation.
  • Open cabinet doorsLet warm house air reach pipes on exterior walls.
  • Let a tap dripA small trickle keeps water moving in at-risk lines.
  • Cover the irrigation backflow deviceIf one sits above ground, wrap it.
  • Know your shutoffLocate the main so you can use it quickly.

If a pipe splits

A frozen pipe may split quietly and only show the damage when it thaws. If water appears or pressure drops after a cold night, close the main valve and call. Our 24/7 line and burst pipe repair page explain what happens next.

Do not use an open flame to thaw a pipe. A hair dryer on low is safer, and only if you have shut off the water.

Water heaters on cold nights

A water heater works harder when incoming water is colder. You may notice the tank running more often. A heater in an exposed garage is better insulated with a blanket made for it, where the manufacturer allows. Do not block vents or air intakes.

If the heater stops entirely, see water heater repair.

A winter maintenance checklist

Winter is also a good time to check shutoffs, test the relief valve on the water heater, inspect hoses on the washer and replace any rubber supply line that looks old. Check under sinks for slow leaks and clear yard drains.

After the cold passes

Remove covers when temperatures rise so moisture does not stay trapped. Inspect hose bibs and outdoor fittings for drips. A fixture that was fine yesterday may now leak, because ice can crack a fitting without bursting it.

Hurricane season is the bigger plumbing season

For plumbing here, wind and flood matter more than frost. Before a storm, know where your main shutoff is, charge your phone and fill a few containers with water in case service is interrupted. After a storm, check the meter and look for wet spots before you assume everything is fine.

Debris in yard drains and cleanouts can back water toward the house. Clearing leaves and sand from them before heavy rain helps water leave your yard instead of pooling at the slab.

A short seasonal checklist

  • SpringCheck hose bibs and irrigation for winter damage.
  • SummerClear yard drains and test the sump if you have one.
  • FallFlush the water heater and inspect supply lines.
  • WinterCover exposed pipes ahead of the rare cold night.
  • Any seasonKnow your shutoffs and where the leaks hide.

What to do if you are traveling during a cold snap

A house left empty for a few days is the riskiest one, because nobody is home to notice a drip or a split. Before leaving, shut the main water valve if you will be gone more than a few days, and ask a neighbor to check the property if lows are forecast in the 30s.

If you keep the water on, set the thermostat at a normal setting so the house does not get cold inside. Open the cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls, and make sure irrigation controllers are set to skip a cold night.

Pool and outdoor equipment lines

Pool equipment sits outdoors, with pipes, valves and heaters exposed to the air. Pool service companies usually give cold-night guidance for the pump and filter, and many pumps have a freeze protection setting that runs the pump when temperatures drop. Follow your pool company's advice.

Outdoor kitchens, outdoor showers and hose bibs on exposed walls are the other weak spots. If you have an outdoor shower, drain it ahead of a forecast cold night and cover the valve.
