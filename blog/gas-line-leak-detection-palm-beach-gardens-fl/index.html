Gas Line Leak Detection for Palm Beach Gardens, FL Homes

If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Do not look for the leak yourself, and do not touch light switches or phones inside.

This guide explains the signs, what detection involves and why a plumber and the utility each have a role.

Emergency first steps

Smell gas? Do this first

Natural gas has an added odor, often described as rotten eggs, so leaks can be noticed. A strong smell, a hissing sound or a dead patch of grass over a buried line are all warning signs.

The right response is the same each time: get out and call from a safe place.

  • Leave the house with everyone and pets. Do not stop to open windows.
  • Do not switch lights, appliances or phones on or off inside.
  • From outside, call the gas utility or 911. The city lists Florida Public Utilities at 561-832-0872 and TECO Peoples Gas at 877-832-6747.
  • Wait outside until the utility says it is safe to return.

Do not relight pilots or run a leak test with a flame. A plumber can inspect after the utility has made the area safe.

What leak detection involves

After the utility has secured the supply, a plumber can pressure test the gas piping inside the house. The test holds a set pressure on the system and watches for a drop. A drop means a leak somewhere in the pipes or fittings.

Finding the exact spot can use a combustible gas detector and a soapy water solution that bubbles at a leak. We do not rely on smell alone, and we do not use open flame. Utility safety guidelines tell customers to leave the building and call from a safe place, and we repeat that advice because it is the right one.

Local note

Emergency numbers listed by the city

The City of Palm Beach Gardens publishes emergency contacts that include Florida Public Utilities at 561-832-0872 for gas, TECO Peoples Gas at 877-832-6747, FPL at 800-468-8243 and Palm Beach County Emergency Management at 561-712-6400.

Which gas company serves you depends on your address. Check your bill and save the number now, so that you are not hunting for it during an emergency.

The same advice applies in North Palm Beach, where the village has its own non-emergency numbers.

Signs worth taking seriously

  • Rotten egg odorThe added odorant is meant to alert you.
  • Hissing near a pipe or applianceEscaping gas makes a sound.
  • Dead plants over a buried lineGas can kill vegetation above a leak.
  • Pilot lights that go out oftenThis can indicate a supply or venting problem.
  • Yellow burner flameGas appliances should burn blue.

Where a plumber helps

Plumbers handle the piping inside the house and its connections to water heaters, ranges and dryers. The utility handles the meter and the service line outside. Splitting responsibility this way means each person works on the piece they are trained to repair.

If you are installing a gas water heater, a range or a tankless unit, we pressure test the connections. See water heater installation and tankless water heater service.

Prevent trouble before it starts

Have gas appliances serviced on a schedule. Keep the area around the water heater and furnace clear of storage. Install a gas detector near gas appliances if you wish, and test it as directed. Know where your gas shutoff is.

After a hurricane or storm

Storms can shift soil and damage buried lines or meters. After a major storm, if you smell gas or see damage near the meter, treat it as an emergency and call the utility. Do not turn the supply back on yourself.

What a pressure test tells you

A gas pressure test is a controlled check done by a qualified person with the supply secured. It shows whether the piping holds pressure over a set time. It does not by itself say where the leak is. Finding the spot needs a second step with a detector or a leak solution on suspect joints.

A passed test is a snapshot. It says the piping held at that moment. It does not replace regular appliance service, and it does not replace a detector in a home with several gas appliances.

Habits that lower the risk

  • Keep appliance areas clearDo not store paint, rags or cleaners near the water heater or furnace.
  • Service appliances on scheduleBurners and connectors wear out.
  • Replace old flex connectorsCorroded or kinked connectors are a common leak point.
  • Learn the shutoffKnow where the gas shutoff is and how it turns.
  • Teach the householdEveryone should know to leave first and call from outside.
