Emergency first steps

Slab leak: what to do right now

A slab leak can lift flooring, undermine soil and raise the bill quickly. Hot water leaks under a slab can also warm the floor.

Stop the water supply while you decide what to do.

  • Close the main water shutoff at the house or meter.
  • Turn off the water heater to protect it from running dry.
  • Move rugs and furniture off any wet or warm floor area.
  • Call the 24/7 line. Tell us about warm floor spots, hissing or a moving meter.

If the main valve will not close, call us immediately. The utility meter shutoff is another option.

Symptoms

Signs that point under the slab

A slab leak announces itself in several ways. A warm spot on tile with no heating system points to a hot line. A constant hiss when all fixtures are off suggests a pressurized break. Cracks in tile or grout lines that follow a line can also be a sign.

None of these alone is proof. We confirm with a pressure test and acoustic locating before recommending any repair, because cutting concrete in the wrong place is costly. The IPC and UPC both cap static water pressure at 80 psi for most buildings, so a pressure reading is part of every slab leak visit.

Causes

Why slab leaks happen

Pipes under a slab can fail from corrosion, abrasion against concrete or soil movement. High water pressure speeds the damage. Copper pipes can develop pinholes over decades, and soil conditions or chemical interaction can play a part.

South Florida's sandy soil and the water table near the surface mean a leak can run for a while before a surface sign shows. That is why a high bill is often the first clue and why we start with the meter.

Insurance

Insurance and documentation

A sudden slab leak may be covered for the damage, while the repair of the pipe itself may not be. Policies vary, so ask your agent which parts apply. We document the leak location with notes and photos you can use for a claim.

Do not start demolition before the insurer has a chance to see the area unless water is causing active harm. Mitigation, such as turning off the main, is expected of you.
