Location

Planning an installation

A new installation starts with where the heater will live. A garage gives room and simple drainage. A closet saves space but needs ventilation and a drain path. An attic location adds a flood risk and requires a secondary pan with a drain.

We look at where the pipes already run, where venting can exit and where a relief line can drain safely. Moving a heater far from the old spot costs more, so we price those options side by side. Gas heater venting follows the maker's installation guidelines and the Florida fuel gas code, and we install to both.

Local conditions

Planning around Florida conditions

South Florida garages hold heat and moisture year round, and that speeds corrosion on tank fittings. A heater near the garage door can also pick up wind-blown rain during storms. We choose locations and materials with that in mind.

The Florida Building Code, Plumbing, 8th Edition (2023) is the current plumbing code. Local permit steps can differ by property, so ask the Palm Beach Gardens Building Division at 561-799-4210. We can help you understand what questions to ask.

Safety

Gas lines and safety: what to do if you smell gas

Gas heaters need a properly sized gas line, a shutoff within reach and a tested connection. If you ever smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Florida Public Utilities lists 561-832-0872 and TECO Peoples Gas lists 877-832-6747.

We pressure test gas connections and check venting after every gas install. A good install should never smell of gas or leave soot at the draft hood.
