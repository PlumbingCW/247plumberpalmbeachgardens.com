Emergency first steps

What to do when a hidden leak is an emergency

A leak you can hear or see spreading is already doing damage. Water under a slab can undermine soil, and water in a wall invites mold.

Stop the supply, then find the source.

  • Shut the main water valve at the house or the meter if water is running or spraying.
  • Turn off the water heater if hot lines are involved.
  • Move belongings away from wet floors and walls.
  • Call the 24/7 line and describe where the damp, hissing or warm spot is.

If a gas line may be involved, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Do not look for the leak yourself near gas.

First check

How to check whether you have a leak

Turn off every tap and appliance, then look at the water meter. If the dial or low-flow indicator keeps moving, water is leaving the system somewhere. Also check toilets with a few drops of food dye in the tank, because a silent flapper leak is a common reason for a high bill.

Seacoast Utility Authority is the water provider for much of Palm Beach Gardens, and its customers can ask the utility about a usage spike. Your own meter reading is the evidence we need to know whether the problem is inside the house or on the line from the meter. According to the EPA's WaterSense program, the average household wastes nearly 10,000 gallons of water a year to leaks.

Signs

Signs of a hidden leak

  • Rising bill with no change in habitsA steady leak can double a bill over a month.
  • Moving meter with everything offThis is the clearest sign.
  • Warm spot on a slab floorA hot water line may be leaking underneath.
  • Hissing in walls or floorsPressurized water leaves a sound.
  • Musty smell or stained drywallWater has been in the wall for some time.
  • Low pressure at tapsA leak between the meter and the house can drop pressure.

Water bill

Reading a high water bill

A jump in a bill is not always a leak. A filled pool, new irrigation settings or visiting family can all raise usage. We start by confirming whether the meter moves with everything off, then split the system to find which section leaks.

Irrigation systems are a frequent culprit in this area, since a broken sprinkler head can run unnoticed. We check the line to the yard when the house side tests dry.

In Palm Beach Gardens

Leaks in Palm Beach Gardens homes

Palm Beach Gardens sits at about 16 feet above sea level and gets about 66 inches of rain a year, so the ground around slabs is often damp. That makes a slab leak harder to notice, because wet soil can look normal. A pressure test removes the guessing.

Older homes may have copper or galvanized supply lines that corrode with age. Newer homes may use PEX or CPVC. Pipe type affects how a leak behaves and how it is repaired, so we note it on every visit.
