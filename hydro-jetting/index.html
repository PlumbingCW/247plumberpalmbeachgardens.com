How jetting differs

Why a cable only helps for a while

A cable punches a hole through a blockage, but it leaves the coating on the pipe wall. In a kitchen line that coating is grease. In an older cast iron or clay line it is scale and fine roots. The hole fills in again, and the clog returns.

Hydro jetting works differently. A nozzle sends water backward and forward at high pressure, and the spray strips the wall clean along the full length of the run. The pipe returns closer to its full diameter, which is the real fix for repeat clogs. Jetting equipment makers publish pressure and nozzle guidelines by pipe type, and we follow those guidelines instead of using full pressure on every line.

Emergency first steps

What to do when a repeat clog becomes an emergency

A line that backs up the same week you had it cabled is reaching the point where the house cannot drain. Jetting can often be done on the same visit once the camera confirms the pipe can take pressure.

If wastewater is already entering the home, call first. We can stabilize the line and explain whether jetting is safe for that pipe.

  • Stop using water in the house to avoid pushing more wastewater into the line.
  • Keep people and pets out of any room with standing sewage.
  • Note which fixtures back up and in what order, because that points to where the blockage is.
  • Call the 24/7 line and ask whether the camera can go first.

We never jet blind. The camera confirms the pipe material and condition before any pressure is applied.

Process

What a jetting visit includes

A good jetting job is camera-first and camera-last.

Camera first

We inspect the line to see grease, roots and any cracks or offsets.

Choose the nozzle

Different nozzles cut roots, scour scale or flush debris downline.

Jet the full run

We work from the cleanout toward the main and back, controlling pressure to match the pipe.

Camera last

We record the clean line and flag anything that needs repair.

Best fits

Lines that benefit most from jetting

  • Restaurant and commercial kitchen linesGrease builds fast on kitchen branches. See our notes on commercial plumbing for grease handling.
  • Older cast iron or clay drainsScale narrows the pipe over decades, so the cable opens a hole in a thick coating.
  • Lines with fine root hairsRoots enter at joints and keep growing back unless the wall is cleaned and any joint defect is repaired.
  • Long runs to the streetA long lateral collects debris that a short cable cannot reach.

Safety

Is jetting safe for my pipes

Hydro jetting is safe on sound pipe, and the camera tells us whether the line is sound. We do not jet a pipe that is cracked, belled out or collapsed, because pressure could make a break worse.

When the camera shows damage, we say so plainly and point to repair options. When it shows a clean-walled line with a single blockage, we may only need a cable. Telling you which is the point of looking first.
