Emergency

Is your North Palm Beach plumbing emergency urgent?

Not every plumbing problem needs a midnight call, but some do. Active water, a sewage backup or a gas smell should not wait until morning.

If you are unsure, call and describe it. We will tell you whether it can wait.

  • Water spraying or pooling: close the main shutoff and call.
  • Sewage in a tub or floor drain: stop all water use and call.
  • No hot water with a wet floor: shut the cold valve above the heater.
  • Gas smell: leave and call the gas utility or 911 from outside.

Village history

About North Palm Beach and what it means for plumbing

North Palm Beach was incorporated on August 13, 1956, after John D. MacArthur bought roughly 2,600 acres in 1954. The village won a national builders' award as a best planned community of 1956, and the North Palm Beach Country Club, with a Jack Nicklaus signature course, opened in 1963.

Those dates matter at the pipe level. Homes from the 1950s and 1960s are likelier than newer ones to have cast iron drains and older supply lines, and a house of that age deserves a camera look when it clogs more than once.

The 2020 census counted 13,162 residents and 8,023 housing units, so the village is compact. Most calls come from within a few miles of US 1 and Northlake Boulevard, which makes dispatch from our Military Trail base straightforward. The Florida Building Code is a statewide standard, and the village building department can confirm what applies to your address.

The ground

Water, soil and ground near the Intracoastal

At about 7 feet above sea level, North Palm Beach sits lower than Palm Beach Gardens, which averages about 16 feet. Lower ground can drain more slowly after heavy rain, so yard drains and cleanouts deserve a check before storm season.

The Seacoast Utility Authority supplies water here, and its 2025 report mentions neighborhood piping projects in North Palm Beach along with Lake Park, Juno Isle and Horseshoe Acres. If work near you changes pressure or clouds the water, call us to check your side of the meter.
