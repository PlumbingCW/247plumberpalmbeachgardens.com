Emergency first steps

What to do when plumbing fails after hours

A burst line, a backed-up restroom or a flooded kitchen can close a business for the day. After-hours calls need two things: stopping the damage and telling us about access.

Tell us if there is a lockbox or a manager who can let us in.

  • Close the main water valve and turn off the water heater if lines are flooding.
  • Keep customers and staff out of wet or contaminated areas.
  • Photograph conditions for your insurer.
  • Call the 24/7 line and tell us the building type and access arrangements.

Restaurants should also tell us whether the problem is in the kitchen line, the restroom or the grease interceptor.

Grease

Grease and the interceptor

Many food businesses have a grease interceptor that catches fats before they enter the sewer. If it is full, grease passes through and coats the line. We clear lines beyond the interceptor but we do not pump interceptors, so you should schedule that with a hauler.

Keeping a pumping schedule and logging it protects you if the utility asks. The utility, not us, sets the requirements, so ask them about yours. Grease interceptor rules come from the local utility's pretreatment standards, so ask the utility what applies to your kitchen.

Preparedness

Keeping a record for your property

Commercial properties benefit from a simple plumbing file. Note the location of the main shutoff, the backflow assembly, the water heater and the cleanouts. Photograph them and label them. Update the file after each service visit.

When an after-hours call comes, staff who can find those items in minutes save the business time and money. We can walk through the building with you on a scheduled visit and mark them.
