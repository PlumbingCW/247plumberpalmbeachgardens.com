Emergency first steps

What to do when a service line breaks

A water line break between the meter and the house can wash out soil, flood a yard and leave you without water. It also wastes water at your expense.

Stopping the flow comes first.

  • Close the main valve at the house. If water still flows, the break is before the valve.
  • The utility meter shutoff may be used with the right key. Do not force a utility valve.
  • Mark the wet area with a stake or flag for the crew.
  • Call the 24/7 line and describe where the water surfaces.

If the break is on the utility side of the meter, we help you contact the utility, Seacoast Utility Authority for many local customers, with the facts.

Responsibility

Who owns which part of the line

Utilities usually own the line up to and including the meter, and the owner is responsible for the pipe from the meter to the house. Rules differ by utility and address. If you are not sure, we find the break first, then tell you which side of the meter it is on.

That avoids repairing something that belongs to the utility and also prevents a long wait on something that does not. Call the utility's customer service for ownership questions about a specific meter. Florida's Underground Facility Damage Prevention and Safety Act, Chapter 556, sets the standard that a locate request goes through Sunshine 811 before anyone digs.

Causes

Why service lines fail in this area

Palm Beach Gardens has homes from many decades, and service lines can be copper, galvanized steel, polyethylene or PVC. Galvanized steel corrodes inside and loses flow. Polyethylene can fail at fittings. Tree roots and nearby digging also damage lines.

The Seacoast Utility Authority reports total chloramine averaging 3.7 ppm in its 2025 report, within the 4 ppm limit. That level is normal for treated drinking water. It is not itself a reason to replace a service line, but old galvanized pipe is.
