Emergency first steps

Toilet overflowing: stop it now

An overflowing toilet pours wastewater onto the floor, and the water is unsanitary. The quick action is to cut the refill and stop flushing.

If other fixtures are also slow, the main line may be involved.

  • Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down, or close the supply valve behind the toilet by turning it clockwise.
  • Stop flushing and ask others in the house to stop running water.
  • Use towels to contain the water and keep children and pets away.
  • Call the 24/7 line and tell us if other fixtures are also slow.

If a toilet and a shower back up together, the cause is likely the main line. Read about sewer line cleaning.

Problems

Common toilet problems

  • Constant runningA worn flapper or fill valve lets water leak into the bowl. Chloramine-treated water can wear rubber parts over time.
  • Weak flushClogged rim jets, a low tank level or a partial blockage.
  • Leak at the baseA failed wax ring or loose closet bolts.
  • Tank sweating or leakingCondensation, loose tank bolts or a cracked tank.
  • Wobbling toiletLoose flange or bolts, sometimes a rotted floor.

Installation

Installing a new toilet

A new toilet install starts with a check of the flange and the floor. A flange that sits too low or a rotted subfloor needs repair first. We set the toilet on a new wax ring or sealing gasket and tighten the bolts evenly.

Rough-in distance, the measure from the wall to the drain center, decides which toilets fit. Most are twelve inches but some are ten or fourteen. We measure before you buy. Federal standards limit new toilets to 1.6 gallons per flush, and WaterSense labeled models use 1.28 gallons or less.

Toilets in Palm Beach Gardens homes

Seacoast Utility Authority tells customers not to flush wipes, even those labeled flushable, because they clog sewer lines and damage pumps. That advice applies to your own toilet and drain, where wipes are one of the most common causes of repeat clogs.

Many local homes have slab floors, so a leak at the base may be seeping into the slab rather than showing as a puddle. A toilet that rocks, or a stain around the base, deserves a look.
