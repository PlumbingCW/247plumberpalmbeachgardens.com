Emergency first steps

What to do when a faucet floods the cabinet

A failed supply line or a broken faucet body can release a lot of water under a sink. The cabinet floor soaks up water and hides the damage.

Find the shutoffs and close them.

  • Turn the small oval-handled valves under the sink clockwise to stop supply.
  • If they will not turn, close the main water valve.
  • Empty the cabinet and dry the area.
  • Call the 24/7 line and tell us if the valves worked.

Stuck shutoff valves are common in older homes. We replace them during the visit.

Types

Kitchen versus bathroom faucets

Kitchen faucets run harder, with pull-down sprayers and hot water used frequently. They fail at the hose and the cartridge. Bathroom faucets fail at the cartridge and the pop-up drain linkage.

Outdoor hose bibs fail differently. A cracked or dripping bib can send water into the wall behind it. We replace the bib and add a shutoff when needed. Federal standards limit bathroom faucets to 2.2 gallons per minute, and WaterSense models use 1.5 or less.

Water quality and faucet wear

Seacoast Utility Authority reports chloramine averaging 3.7 ppm in its 2025 water quality report, and the report lists no hardness figure. Chloramine can wear rubber seals over time, which is one reason washers and O-rings fail. If you see white scale on fixtures, test your tap or ask the utility about hardness before choosing a treatment.

We note fixture condition when we replace parts, so you know if wear is routine or a sign of something else.

Replacing

Choosing a replacement faucet

Faucets differ in how they mount and how many holes they need. Measure the sink holes, the spacing and the depth of the cabinet below. A pull-down kitchen faucet needs room for the hose to travel, and a tall spout may not clear a window sill.

Look at the valve type inside. Ceramic disc cartridges are common, and replacement cartridges are easy to find. Cheaper faucets with plastic internals may be hard to repair later. We install the faucet you choose and test it under full flow.
